What is Blue Aluminum?

Aluminum whose function, engineering context, and material lifecycle are materially connected to a blue-economy system.

The compact definition

Blue Aluminum is aluminum deployed in products, infrastructure, or operational systems that enable sustainable ocean-linked economic activity—and whose claims are bounded by evidence about material, use, conditions, and lifecycle.

The noun is the material. “Blue” describes the economic system it serves, not an automatic environmental virtue.

What the definition does

It creates a shared category across industries that currently describe the same material relationship in disconnected language: marine aluminum, offshore structures, port systems, aquaculture equipment, coastal works, and ocean technology.

What it does not do

It does not certify low carbon, responsible sourcing, corrosion performance, or recyclability. Those are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

Five-question admission test
01

Blue-economy function

Does the application perform a necessary or material role in an ocean-linked economic system?

02

Material specificity

Is aluminum’s selection connected to defined engineering or lifecycle requirements?

03

Operating context

Are marine, coastal, offshore, or port conditions described rather than implied?

04

Claim evidence

Can performance, sourcing, and impact claims be traced to appropriate evidence?

05

Lifecycle boundary

Are fabrication, maintenance, service life, and recovery considered where material?
